# User-test plan

**Status: not yet conducted.** No participants, results or learning gains exist yet.

## Goal
Find out whether undergraduates can (1) compare viruses correctly and (2) interpret the schematic diagrams without over-reading them.

## Participants
5–8 undergraduates who have taken at least intro biology. Recruit informally (study groups); no personal data beyond year and major is recorded.

## Format
30 minutes, think-aloud, one facilitator, screen recording only with consent. Local build on a laptop; half the sessions on a phone.

## Tasks (record correct/incorrect and time)
1. Find a virus that has a capsid but no envelope. (Expected: adenovirus 5, HPV16 or T4.)
2. Using Compare, name two differences between influenza A and SARS-CoV-2 genomes. (Sense; segmentation.)
3. On the HSV-1 profile, identify the tegument and say what it is.
4. In the size explorer, say whether the T4 head is longer or wider, and by roughly how much. (Longer: 120 vs 86 nm.)
5. Switch to the logarithmic view and explain why the bars can't be compared by length.
6. Find the source behind the SARS-CoV-2 diameter and say whether it is a range or a mean ± SD.
7. "Are HSV-1 and adenovirus closely related because both are dsDNA?" Answer using the atlas.
8. Point to one thing a diagram does *not* show.

## Questions after tasks
- What, if anything, was confusing about the evidence labels (verified / simplified / variable / not verified)?
- Did any diagram look more precise than it is?
- System Usability Scale (10 items).

## Analysis
Report task success counts as raw numbers (e.g. "4 of 6"), quotes, and SUS scores. Do not claim learning gains without a pre/post design.
