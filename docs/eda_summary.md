# Exploratory Analysis — GB1 Single Mutants

Generated by `scripts/run_eda.py`. Figures in `docs/figures/`.

## 1. Score distribution (`01_score_distribution.png`)

Most single substitutions cluster near or above WT (score ≈ 0), with a
left-skewed tail of strongly deleterious mutations. This shape is typical of
protein fitness landscapes: most positions tolerate most substitutions,
while a minority of positions are intolerant to nearly everything.

**What it suggests:** GB1 has a mix of structurally forgiving and
structurally critical positions.
**What it cannot establish:** the histogram alone can't say *why* a mutation
is deleterious (folding vs. binding vs. expression) — see
`docs/dataset_provenance.md` for why this assay conflates those mechanisms.

## 2. Position × substitution heatmap (`02_position_substitution_heatmap.png`)

Coverage is complete except for the 55 diagonal "self" cells (WT residue to
itself isn't a mutation, so it's structurally absent from the data, not
missing-at-random). This is the "assessment of missing-data coverage": there
is no missing data to characterize beyond that expected structural absence.

Two patterns jump out:
- **Horizontal dark-blue bands** at positions 26, 30, 40, 42, 51 — almost
  every substitution tested there is strongly deleterious. These are
  candidates for structurally/functionally critical positions (e.g. buried
  core residues or direct IgG-contact residues).
- **A near-uniform deleterious column at proline (P)** across most
  positions — consistent with proline's known tendency to disrupt
  regular secondary structure by removing the backbone NH needed for
  typical hydrogen bonding and by rigidifying the backbone.

**What it cannot establish:** which specific mechanism (core packing,
binding interface, backbone geometry) explains any one dark cell. That
requires structural context (see Section 9 / optional structural extension),
and even then, proximity in a structure is a hypothesis generator, not proof
of mechanism.

## 3. Effects by amino-acid property (`03_property_effects.png`)

Mutations that change charge category and mutations that make a residue much
more polar (large negative Δhydrophobicity) both skew toward lower scores
than charge-preserving / hydrophobicity-preserving substitutions.

**What it suggests:** some of GB1's fitness landscape is explainable by
simple physicochemical rules, which is exactly what the linear model in
Section 6 is testing formally.
**What it cannot establish:** correlation between a property bin and score
is not proof that *that property* is the causal mechanism — confounded by
which positions happen to carry which WT residues.

## 4. Positions with consistently large effects (`04_large_effect_positions.png`)

Using the assay's own documented binarization convention (`lnW < -4`,
ProteinGym's recorded `DMS_binarization_cutoff` for this exact assay) as the
definition of "large effect," positions **26, 30, 40, 42, 51** have at least
half of their 19 possible substitutions classified as large-effect. This
reuses an assay-anchored threshold rather than an arbitrary one chosen by
this project.

**What it cannot establish:** "large effect" here is specific to *this*
binding/stability-conflated assay and cutoff — it is not a general statement
that these positions matter for GB1's biological function outside the assay,
and it says nothing about any other protein.

## 5. Replicate agreement

Not applicable. `SPG1_STRSG_Olson_2014` is one pooled mRNA-display selection
followed by deep sequencing — there is no second, independent biological
replicate in this dataset to compare against. This is stated explicitly
rather than silently skipped, per `docs/dataset_provenance.md`.
